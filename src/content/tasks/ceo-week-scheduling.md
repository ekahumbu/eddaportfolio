## The brief

Dana's standing rules: deep work, protected lunch, buffers after long meetings, a Thursday 4:45 PM hard stop, no COO 1:1 cancelled two weeks running, and vendors routed to Operations. External messages need her approval. She is flying 3:00–5:00 PM ET and wants one short summary.

Deadlines: Kevin Cho (TrailMart, the number one account) locks his calendar at 8:00 PM ET today. Ellen Shaw needs the board answer by Monday 12:00 PM ET.

## My approach

1. **Log.** Listed every request and every clash, rule risk and knock-on.
2. **Rank.** Ranked by priority: board and investor first, then TrailMart, then the VP hire, leadership, 1:1s and vendors.
3. **Resolve.** For each conflict, compared two options and recorded the choice, the reason and the rule.
4. **Rebuild.** Rebuilt the week and checked it for overlaps and the Thursday 4:45 stop.
5. **Communicate.** Drafted each message, split by send now and needs Dana, plus one short email to Dana.

The hardest call: sending Kevin a time-only offer under Dana's "protect that call" text instead of waiting for her to land. I flagged it to her as an FYI.

## What I delivered

One workbook with seven sheets:

- **Requests:** the nine incoming requests, converted to ET and ranked.
- **Conflict log:** every clash, rule risk and dependency, by ID (C01 to C18).
- **Working calendar:** the original week with conflicts marked.
- **Resolution log:** options, decision and reasoning for each conflict.
- **Adjusted calendar:** the final week.
- **Messages:** outgoing messages and their status.
- **Dana summary:** the email to Dana, under 200 words.

## What I'd do with more time

Check every time in every message against the calendar before sending. I'd also keep the workbook simpler from the start.
